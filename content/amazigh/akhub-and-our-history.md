ⴰⵅⵓⴱ (pronounced "Akhub" in Tamazight) denotes a mix of melancholy and agony. This single word describes much of the history of Amazigh people in North Africa, at best it can be seen as a fractured, unrecorded history, and a continuous series of occupations and exploitations. First came the Romans, followed by their successors: the Byzantines and the Germanic Vandals. When our ancestors finally began to stand up, their authentic identity was repeatedly driven underground, obscured beneath exogenous layers, but here we are sharper than Obsidian and taller than Cedrus.

Yes, ⴰⵅⵓⴱ is a mirror of our entire history against external forces forever attempting to scratch away our existence. Sometimes I feel it—I mean *Akhub*—directed toward ancestors who left us so few records, or when I strive to forge a connection with them only to fall short, like a lone tree leaf trying to reach down to dead roots.

I was reminded of this word by a poem I stumbled upon among countless YouTube videos: [Moha Bibi & Elhoucine Elaziz on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ZIh8asqhmg) [00:00:00]. It captures our status as Imazighen melted down in a pot of outside identities too numerous to count. The lyrics are by poet Elhoucine Elaziz; whose digital footprint I failed to find, mirroring my struggle to connect with my ancestors;and sung by Moha Bibi, a renowned Outra instrument player and poet who composed songs for the legendary Rouicha.

Here is my humble attempt to translate the poem:

> To the one crying over there,  
> To the one crying over there:  
> Is it the agony that tears you apart  
> The agony of the Amazigh people
> That you are crying over?  
> *(Repeated)*  
> 
> Stop crying, the blame rests on your shoulders;  
> You are the one who brought anguish to the Amazigh people.  
> *(Repeated twice)*  
> 
> You are the one who neglected the land,  
> Who left the Imazighen to dwell high in the mountains and deep in the forests.  
> *(Repeated twice)*
