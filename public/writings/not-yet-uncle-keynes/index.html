Not Yet, Uncle Keynes

November 13, 2026

In 1930, John Maynard Keynes looked a century ahead and promised his grandchildren a utopia. With technology doing the heavy work, he figured we’d all be working 15-hour weeks, leaving the rest of our days open for philosophy, art, and actually living in the moment.

Nearly a hundred years later, the verdict is in: Not yet, Uncle Keynes.

Instead of a golden age of leisure, we got burnout culture on steroids and humans fueling on Monster energy drinks. The machines didn’t set us free; they just built a compact, quantum cage.

The Hamster Wheel Economy#

Keynes thought humanity’s biggest crisis in 2030 would be figuring out what to do with too much free time. Instead, our modern crisis is a relentless, soul-crushing grind.

Look at the numbers: the world’s 12 richest billionaires have more wealth than the poorest half of humanity (over four billion people combined); let that sink in. Twelve human beings hoard more resources than 4,000,000,000 others. Meanwhile, ordinary people are caught in a perpetual spin cycle, running like guinea pigs just to pay rent and mortgages, and to afford basic education and healthcare for their kids. No one lives in the present moment anymore because every single second of our lives has been mortgaged to keep the system afloat.

Factories of Obedience#

Keynes imagined education would evolve to teach us the “art of living.” Instead, our schools and universities remain industrial assembly lines designed to train compliant, overworked robots.

From day one, you are conditioned not to question the structure of society, but to build a clean resume, take on mountains of student debt, and feed yourself into a corporate machine through co-ops and internships. The entire game is rigged to funnel wealth upward, ensuring that the fruits of our collective labor stay locked in the hands of an untouchable corporate elite.

Clowns Instead of Philosophers#

In Keynes’s vision, social prestige belonged to artists, poets, and thinkers. Look around today. Our most famous, wealthy, and hyper-exposed public figures aren’t philosophers, they are entertainers, influencers, and social media clowns.

And can you blame people for tuning in? When your daily reality is an exhausting treadmill of survival, you don’t have the mental bandwidth for deep systemic critique. People lean into mindless digital escapism not because they are lazy, but because they are trying to anesthetize themselves against a reality they feel completely powerless to change.

A Rigged World of Hate and Division#

Capitalism didn’t become kinder, Uncle John. It became more aggressive, spreading its tentacles into every corner of the globe. When an economic system pushes billions of people to the breaking point while a handful of tech oligarchs buy up social media platforms and throw trillion-dollar parties, the system has to protect itself. How? By manufacturing hate between the masses.

Instead of letting us look upward at the billionaires cashing out on everyone’s sweat, the political and media elite point fingers downward; horizontally instead of vertically. They weaponize prejudice, fund far-right divisions, and spark geopolitical conflicts, wars, and genocides to keep the working class fighting itself. We are governed by a rotating cast of political actors who serve corporate donors while the planet burns.

Uncle Keynes looked at the dawn of modern productivity and saw liberation. He failed to foresee that corporate greed would capture the state, rewrite the rules, and turn human potential into a spreadsheet metric measured by AI systems.

The future Keynes promised isn’t canceled; it’s just being held hostage by the wealthy. Until we break the cage of extreme inequality, Keynes’s paradise will remain a ghost story of a future that never arrived. And probably we never will, the bird who grew up in a cage will always return to it; the elephant who grew up in chains will not break them even when it is bigger.
